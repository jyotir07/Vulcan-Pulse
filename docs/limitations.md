# Limitations

Read every number in this project with these in mind.

## What this is not

1. **The data is synthetic.** Every transaction, merchant, customer and outcome is generated by
   `backend/data`. Bank, gateway and city names are labels; nothing describes the real
   institutions or real Indian payment infrastructure.
2. **Results do not represent real Razorpay payment performance**, or anyone's. Success rates, GMV
   and latencies are properties of the generator's configuration.
3. **This is not Vulcan.** It is inspired by the publicly described idea behind Razorpay's Vulcan,
   a shared representation learned from structured payment data. It does not reproduce or use
   Razorpay's proprietary model, data, infrastructure or algorithms.

## What the numbers depend on

4. **The simulator has known assumptions**, all documented in `docs/dgp.md`: a staged failure
   process, quadratic load saturation, one retry, issuer reliability that does not depend on the
   gateway, a fixed set of episode types. A different world would give different results.
5. **Counterfactual estimates are only as good as the data-generating process and the model.** The
   ground truth is exact *for this simulator*; it validates the engine and the models against the
   world they were built for, not against reality.
6. **Real payment systems have factors the simulation leaves out**: customer behaviour changing in
   response to failures beyond one retry, merchant-side routing logic, network partitions,
   fraud adaptations, regulatory limits, settlement, and correlated failures across issuers.

## Known weaknesses the evaluation found

7. **Learned models barely respond to health-driven interventions.** Degraded states are rare in
   training and the observed health feature is a noisy trailing rate, so neither the trees nor
   the shared representation learns how much success falls when an issuer or gateway degrades.
   The rule baseline, which assumes the relationship, is far more accurate there
   (`docs/evaluation.md`).
8. **The ensemble's intervals are too narrow.** They combine member spread and a block bootstrap,
   but miss the model's systematic bias, so the truth falls outside them far more often than 10%
   of the time (`docs/results.md`). Treat them as a lower bound on uncertainty.
9. **Nonlinear load is under-predicted by everyone.** Scenarios that push gateways past saturation
   (large surges, big reroutes) are where every predictor misses the most.
10. **The engine does not model behaviour changes.** Retries that a scenario would add or remove
    are not predicted; only first attempts are scored.
11. **Attribution describes the predictor's view.** A factor the model ignores gets no credit in
    the Shapley split, even if it matters in the true process.
12. **Mitigations are simulation output.** They are ranked by what the chosen predictor expects,
    over a small candidate set (reroutes and issuer-scoped method steering), not a guaranteed
    real-world effect.

## Operational limits

- The shared ensemble takes over an hour to train on a laptop CPU, and an ensemble simulation
  of a large traffic change takes tens of seconds. The rule baseline answers in about a second.
- Natural-language input needs an LLM key on the server. Without one, the form builder is the
  only input.
